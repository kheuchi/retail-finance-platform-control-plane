## Context

> **TL;DR:** you cannot test a fraud detector without knowing where the fraud is.

Public retail datasets have sales but no general ledger, no budget, and they often carry
customer IDs (PII). Synthetic data with **planted anomalies** gives a ground truth: later,
the reconciliation and the ML models must find exactly those, and nothing else.

## Tricky parts

> **TL;DR:** realism is in the details, and the ledger must reconcile to the cent.

### 1. Clean by construction
- GL revenue is generated **from** POS net sales, converted to EUR with the ECB rate of the
  day. A test asserts they match per store and day. So any break later is a planted one.

### 2. Calendars
- German stores close on Sundays and national holidays; Easter moves every year and is
  computed (anonymous Gregorian algorithm), not hard-coded. Tested.

### 3. Size vs memory
- ~482 MB of CSV, generated month by month (~370 MB RAM, ~2 min locally), so it runs on a
  single small cluster node.

### 4. Linter surprise
- Ruff's autofix added `zip(..., strict=False)`. We changed it to `strict=True`, so
  mismatched lists fail loudly instead of being silently cut.

## Proof

- 8/8 tests: same seed → same data, journals balance, GL = POS, closed days, refund links,
  anomalies match the truth file, budget rule, Easter dates.
- Sanity: 2025 net revenue EUR 38.9m, gross margin 34-35%, returns 1.4%.
